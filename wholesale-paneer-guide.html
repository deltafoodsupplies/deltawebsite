Buyer guide · Dairy

Wholesale paneer buying guide for restaurants and grocery stores.

Paneer is the highest-turnover item in Indian dairy — and one of the easiest to get wrong. Here is how experienced buyers choose formats, plan volumes, and keep waste near zero.

Know your formats

Wholesale paneer comes in three broad formats, and the right mix depends on whether you sell it or cook with it. Retail blocks (typically 12 oz to 1 lb) are what grocery shoppers expect in the dairy case — recognizable brands like Nanak move fastest because customers ask for them by name. Bulk blocks (multi-pound foodservice packs) suit restaurant kitchens that cube paneer daily for curries and tandoori dishes. Pre-cut cubes save prep labor and portion consistently, which matters for high-volume kitchens and caterers pricing by the tray.

Fresh vs. frozen — and why most volume is frozen

Most wholesale paneer in the U.S. moves frozen. Properly frozen paneer holds its texture well for cooking applications, and it turns a three-week product into a months-long one, which is what makes stocking practical outside the biggest metros. Keep frozen paneer at 0°F, thaw under refrigeration overnight, and never refreeze a thawed block. For the softest texture in fresh-style dishes, soak thawed paneer in warm water for 10–15 minutes before use.

Plan volumes around your menu, not your freezer

A practical starting point for restaurants: estimate paneer dishes per week, multiply by your per-dish portion (most kitchens run 4–6 oz per entree), and add 15–20% for specials and catering. A restaurant serving 150 paneer entrees a week uses roughly 40–55 lb — one or two bulk cases. Grocery stores should let the dairy case tell the story: track weekly sell-through per brand for a month before committing to bigger case volumes, and let your distributor carry the inventory risk in between orders.

What separates good paneer at wholesale

Look for a clean milky aroma, uniform color without a dry rind, and a block that slices without crumbling. At the case level, check that frozen blocks arrive rock-solid with no ice crystals inside the packaging — crystals mean a broken cold chain somewhere upstream. This is why buying from a distributor with in-house trucks and a managed cold chain matters more for paneer than for almost any other category.

The short version

Stock recognizable retail brands for grocery, bulk or cubed formats for kitchens, keep the cold chain unbroken, and start with conservative volumes that your sales data — not your freezer size — grows over time.
