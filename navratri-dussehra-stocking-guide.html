Stocking for Navratri and Dussehra.

Navratri is nine nights of devotion, fasting and garba dancing, ending with Dussehra. Gujarati, Marathi and North Indian families buy fasting (farali) foods all week, and garba nights bring snack and drink orders from community organisers.

Order three weeks ahead

Navratri starts in late September or October. Order about three weeks before the first night, then top up mid-week — fasting staples sell steadily for nine days, and Dussehra adds a sweets peak at the end.

The fasting (farali) shelf

  • Sabudana — Indravathi sabudana for khichdi and vada.
  • Makhana and peanuts — Apna phool makhana, GNR jumbo peanuts, peanut ladoos.
  • Farali snacks — Kathiyawadi farali rajgira khakhra and bhakhri, Balaji farali chevdo.
  • Fasting flours and salt — rajgira, kuttu and singhara flour and sendha namak; ask us what is in stock for your route.

Garba nights and Dussehra

Garba organisers order snacks and drinks in bulk: namkeen, khakhra, sharbat and frozen appetizers for the food stalls. For Dussehra, bring in boondi and navrattan ladoos and festival sweets — and remember Diwali orders start right after.

The short version

Order 3 weeks early, build a dedicated farali shelf, top up mid-week, serve garba organisers with bulk snacks, and end with Dussehra sweets. Our team can tell you exactly what is available for your route this season.

Planning your Navratri order?

Ask for farali staples, garba-night snacks and Dussehra sweets for your route.
